# Mechanism Hypotheses

The question “How does remote viewing work?” has three different meanings:

1. **Operational:** what does a viewer actually do during a session?
2. **Phenomenological:** what does the information feel like to the viewer?
3. **Causal:** what process, ordinary or anomalous, produces target correspondence?

The first can be documented. The second can be reported and compared. The third remains unresolved.

## 1. The operational answer

A defensible remote-viewing workflow is an information-control system:

```text
target universe
  → random assignment
  → opaque cue
  → blind viewer session
  → immutable transcript
  → independent judging against decoys
  → feedback
  → complete trial ledger
```

CRV adds a structured sequence intended to keep low-level impressions separate from object-level interpretation. Whether the information is paranormal or ordinary, that separation can reduce hindsight editing and make the output more auditable.

## 2. The CRV practitioner model

Historical CRV materials use terms such as:

- **Matrix:** a proposed repository or totality of information;
- **signal line:** the supposed information-bearing connection between target and viewer;
- **aperture:** the amount or bandwidth of information available at a stage;
- **ideogram:** a rapid spontaneous mark associated with initial site contact;
- **AOL (Analytical Overlay):** object-level naming or story formation imposed on fragmentary impressions;
- **AOL break:** explicitly writing the guess, pausing, and returning to raw descriptors;
- **aesthetic impact (AI):** the viewer’s immediate affective response to the site;
- **movement exercise:** a prompt to change perspective, scale, time, or position.

These terms are useful as a **training grammar**. They are not direct measurements of a Matrix or signal. The method’s strongest testable idea is not its metaphysics; it is that rapid, low-level description may score better than early object naming.

## 3. Why AOL matters even under ordinary psychology

Suppose the first impression is:

```text
cool · hard · smooth · curved · rhythmic sound
```

The mind may name “submarine.” Once that label appears, later marks and words can be unconsciously edited toward it. CRV calls this AOL. Ordinary cognitive science would describe related risks as anchoring, confirmation bias, imagery elaboration, demand characteristics, or source-monitoring error.

The protocol response is the same under either explanation:

```text
AOL — submarine
```

Then return to testable descriptors. The guess is preserved rather than erased, but it is not allowed to masquerade as raw data.

## 4. Competing causal hypotheses

The programme should not ask one experiment to distinguish every explanation. Each hypothesis below has a characteristic prediction.

### H0-A: sensory or procedural leakage

**Claim:** target information reaches the viewer or judge through ordinary cues: filenames, monitor behaviour, room sounds, transcript metadata, target-set familiarity, feedback timing, or accidental access.

**Predictions:**

- performance falls when cue channels are audited and removed;
- results cluster around operators, rooms, software, or target pools;
- transcript sanitation changes judging outcomes;
- target identity may be reconstructed from metadata without using session content.

**Discriminating controls:**

- separate target manager, viewer, monitor, and judge;
- strip dates, coordinate order, handwriting clues, and trial numbers before judging;
- use generated opaque cues unrelated to target properties;
- run adversarial leakage reviews;
- include sham tasks with identical procedure but no target assignment.

### H0-B: flexible matching and judge effects

**Claim:** free-form descriptions appear accurate because a judge can find correspondences after seeing the targets.

**Predictions:**

- different judges produce inconsistent rankings;
- the principal investigator or target-aware judge scores more positively;
- broad, high-base-rate descriptors drive results;
- automated or preregistered feature scoring reduces the effect.

**Discriminating controls:**

- multiple independent blind judges;
- inter-rater reliability;
- fixed descriptor ontology and scoring weights;
- transcript-to-decoy comparison, not target-only commentary;
- leave-one-judge-out analysis.

### H0-C: target-pool and statistical artifacts

**Claim:** unequal target salience, repeated motifs, non-independent trials, optional stopping, post-hoc exclusions, or inappropriate models create apparent significance.

**Predictions:**

- results weaken under balanced target pools and prospective analysis;
- one viewer, judge, target class, or laboratory accounts for most of the effect;
- sensitivity analyses change the conclusion.

**Discriminating controls:**

- pre-register the pool, sample size, exclusions, and primary outcome;
- model viewer, target, judge, and laboratory as separate effects;
- publish all randomization and commitment receipts;
- run robustness and permutation analyses.

### H0-D: memory, feedback, expectancy, and selective reporting

**Claim:** feedback reshapes memory of what was written, while hits are retained and misses are forgotten or unpublished.

**Predictions:**

- post-feedback summaries look more accurate than pre-feedback transcripts;
- confidence rises after suggestive feedback even when objective score does not;
- retrospective case collections outperform prospective ledgers.

**Discriminating controls:**

- hash the transcript and drawings before feedback;
- forbid post-feedback edits to the raw layer;
- register every initiated trial;
- compare contemporaneous score with later recollection;
- publish null and aborted sessions with predeclared status codes.

### H1: sender-mediated telepathy

**Claim:** the viewer obtains information from a person attending to the target rather than directly from the target.

**Predictions:**

- sender-present trials outperform no-sender trials;
- sender attention timing matters;
- selecting the target after the session should eliminate the effect unless a precognitive process also operates.

**Discriminating test:**

Randomize otherwise identical trials among attentive sender, distracted sender, and no-sender conditions. Keep viewer, monitor, judge, target pool, and feedback identical. Neither the viewer nor monitor knows the condition.

### H2: direct target access / clairvoyance

**Claim:** the viewer accesses information about a target that exists and is selected before the session without needing a sender.

**Predictions:**

- no-sender trials remain above chance;
- target properties matter more than who knows the target;
- target-after-session trials should not work unless precognition is also possible.

**Discriminating test:**

Preselect and cryptographically commit a target before the session; ensure no human sender attends to it; compare with an identically managed target selected only after transcript lock.

### H3: precognition of future target or feedback

**Claim:** correspondence is with a future target selection or feedback experience.

**Predictions:**

- trials can score above chance when the target is randomly chosen after the transcript is locked;
- feedback properties and timing may affect results;
- shielding and spatial distance need not matter.

**Discriminating test:**

Lock the transcript first. Only then invoke an independently audited randomizer to select the target. Predeclare feedback timing and prevent rerandomization. Compare immediate, delayed, and no-feedback arms.

### H4: experimenter or decision-augmentation effects

**Claim:** apparent remote-viewing success arises through anomalous or ordinary influence on random choices, trial inclusion, judging, or decisions rather than information transfer to the viewer.

The 1995 **Decision Augmentation Theory** report is the historical formal version of this family of ideas. It treats selection or decision bias as a possible information-mediated account and offers predictions intended to differ from force-like models. In this module, DAT is a hypothesis generator—not an established mechanism. `SRC-CIA-DAT-1995`

**Predictions:**

- results depend strongly on experimenter and decision path;
- changing who initiates randomization or judges the transcript changes outcomes;
- viewer content may be less predictive than analyst choices.

**Discriminating controls:**

- automatic, logged randomization;
- role separation;
- immutable inclusion rules;
- independent reproduction from raw artifacts;
- analyses that ask whether content predicts the target before any human choice can adapt.

### H5: CRV Matrix / signal-line access

**Claim:** an information-bearing “signal line” connects consciousness to a comprehensive Matrix, and CRV stages progressively decode it.

**Predictions that can be operationalized:**

- early low-level data should precede and predict later high-level correspondences;
- stage discipline should outperform equally timed free association;
- AOL-labelled nouns should be less accurate than sensory and spatial descriptors;
- interruption, stage skipping, and analytic naming should reduce performance;
- training should produce a measurable learning curve.

**What would not count as a test:**

- treating any impression as evidence of the Matrix;
- redefining every miss as signal displacement;
- invoking an invisible mechanism only after seeing results;
- comparing trained CRV viewers with completely untrained controls while confounding practice time, motivation, and feedback.

### H6: hybrid or unknown process

The data may reflect more than one process: ordinary leakage in some trials, judge artifacts in others, and an unresolved anomaly in a subset. A good programme permits mixture models rather than forcing one universal story.

## 5. A first-principles research matrix

| Question | Minimal comparison | Primary readout |
|---|---|---|
| Is a sender required? | sender vs no sender | blind rank / target-feature score |
| Must the target exist before viewing? | target-before vs target-after-lock | differential effect |
| Does CRV structure help? | CRV vs matched descriptive-control training | preregistered primary score |
| Does AOL marking help? | required AOL logging vs silent free association | descriptor accuracy and calibration |
| Do multiple viewers help? | one viewer vs fixed-size independent ensemble | out-of-sample aggregate score |
| Is feedback necessary for learning? | immediate vs delayed vs limited feedback | longitudinal slope |
| Are judges driving results? | human judges vs fixed feature model | agreement and effect stability |
| Does target entropy matter? | balanced low- vs high-complexity pools | interaction effect |
| Is confidence calibrated? | confidence recorded before reveal | calibration curve / Brier score |
| Does any effect generalize? | independent multi-lab replication | held-out protocol result |

## 6. What “how it works” can responsibly mean today

At present, the strongest answer is layered:

- **Historically:** CRV works as a disciplined notation and training procedure that moves from rapid gestalt and sensory fragments toward spatial and conceptual detail.
- **Psychologically:** it attempts to control premature interpretation, preserve raw output, and use feedback for learning.
- **Experimentally:** remote viewing is tested through blinding, random target assignment, transcript locking, decoy judging, and repeated trials.
- **Causally:** no mechanism has been established. The AIR review found an unresolved laboratory anomaly but did not identify its cause; later positive syntheses and critical literature remain in tension.
- **Practically:** the only honest way forward is to execute clean trials and design comparisons that make the theories risk different outcomes.

## 7. Falsification and stop rules

A theory that explains every result explains nothing. Before a study, write what would count against the preferred hypothesis.

Examples:

- If CRV does not outperform a time- and feedback-matched control across the preregistered sample, do not claim CRV structure is superior.
- If target-after-lock trials fail while target-before trials succeed, do not claim the result demonstrates precognition.
- If performance disappears after transcript sanitation, investigate leakage before paranormal explanations.
- If a positive effect is carried by one judge or viewer and fails leave-one-out analysis, label it non-generalized.
- If an independent team cannot reproduce the workflow or score from the artifacts, the result is not release-ready.
- If twenty clean pilot trials show no hint of usable signal, pause operational ambitions and improve the design only through changes declared before the next cohort.

The first-principles possibility stance is strongest when it is willing to lose.

# History and Evidence

This document is a source-led map of the remote-viewing record. It is not a vote between believers and skeptics, and it does not treat duration of government interest as proof of efficacy. It asks what each source can actually establish.

Source identifiers such as `SRC-CIA-AIR-1995` resolve in [`source-index.json`](source-index.json).

## 1. Keep four histories separate

Public discussions often collapse four partly overlapping histories:

1. **Government sponsorship and administration** — who funded, transferred, renamed, suspended, or reviewed a programme.
2. **Laboratory research** — controlled target-description experiments and their statistical evaluation.
3. **Operational tasking** — attempts to answer intelligence questions where targets and success criteria were less controlled.
4. **CRV method development** — the training vocabulary and stage structure associated with Ingo Swann, SRI, and military trainees.

A government document proves that an agency created or used a procedure. It does not, by itself, prove the procedure’s paranormal explanation or operational value. Conversely, a negative operational review does not logically erase every laboratory anomaly. The claims must stay separate.

## 2. A cautious programme timeline

The precise organizational genealogy is messy, and public sources sometimes assign different start dates or treat overlapping code words as a single continuous unit. Use the following as a **programme-family map**, not as a claim that one unchanged office operated for twenty-three years.

| Period | What can be said carefully | Primary anchors |
|---|---|---|
| 1972–1977 | CIA sponsored SRI work on anomalous perception and explored intelligence applications; AIR later reported that CIA’s programme began in 1972 and was discontinued in 1977. | `SRC-CIA-AIR-1995`, `SRC-TARG-PUTHOFF-1974`, `SRC-PUTHOFF-TARG-1976` |
| Late 1970s–early 1980s | Army/INSCOM and related defence organizations produced GRILL FLAME reports and training sessions. Declassified files use “raw intelligence data” language and refer to an applied remote-viewing protocol. | `SRC-CIA-CD58-1980`, `SRC-CIA-LIII-1978`, `SRC-CIA-XCI`, `SRC-CIA-876-1982` |
| 1982–mid-1980s | Coordinate/Controlled Remote Viewing training was formalized and recorded through stage notation, sensory data, sketches, AOL, and feedback classifications. | `SRC-CIA-CRVT-1982`, `SRC-CIA-RV018-1985`, `SRC-CRV-MANUAL-1986` |
| Mid-1980s–1991 | DIA involvement and SUN STREAK-era tasking continued. AIR later described DIA’s direct involvement as beginning about 1985. | `SRC-CIA-SUN-STREAK-1987`, `SRC-CIA-AIR-1995` |
| Early 1990s–spring 1995 | STAR GATE became the programme label under which operations, research and development, and foreign assessment were grouped. Operational tasking was suspended in spring 1995. | `SRC-CIA-AIR-1995` |
| 1995 | Congress directed transfer/review; CIA commissioned AIR. AIR recommended against continued intelligence use. The programme was terminated and documents were declassified in stages. | `SRC-CIA-AIR-1995`, `SRC-UTTS-1995`, `SRC-HYMAN-1995` |
| 1996 onward | The AIR dispute, replications, critiques, practitioner schools, archival releases, and new syntheses continued outside the programme. | `SRC-NRC-1988`, `SRC-TRESSOLDI-KATZ-2023`, `SRC-AKIN-TRESSOLDI-KATZ-2026` |

Common labels in the wider programme family include SCANATE, GONDOLA WISH, GRILL FLAME, CENTER LANE, SUN STREAK, and STAR GATE. A later registry-quality chronology should be assembled from dated memoranda rather than copied from unsourced code-name lists.

### 2.1 The Soviet/Russian motivation question

A 1972 U.S. intelligence report, **Controlled Offensive Behavior — USSR**, surveyed reported Soviet work on behavior influence, biocommunications, parapsychology, and possible military use. It is strong evidence that U.S. organizations were collecting and assessing such claims at the start of the American programme era. `SRC-DIA-USSR-1972`

It is not strong evidence that the alleged Soviet effects worked. The report is a U.S. intelligence compilation, not a complete Soviet primary archive or independent replication. “They were studying us while we studied them” can explain Cold War motivation; it cannot, without measured outcomes, establish efficacy.

A future foreign-programme dossier should preserve primary Soviet/Russian records separately from U.S. threat assessments, translations, memoirs, and later retellings.

## 3. What the early laboratory papers claimed

### 3.1 Targ and Puthoff, Nature 1974

The 1974 Nature paper reported experiments in which participants described information not presented to known senses and argued that such effects could be studied in laboratory conditions. Publication establishes that the work entered peer-reviewed scientific debate; it is not a seal that the conclusions were accepted. `SRC-TARG-PUTHOFF-1974`

### 3.2 SRI final research report, 1975

The 155-page **Perceptual Augmentation Techniques Part Two—Research Report** is a primary programme document. It records the investigators’ methods and their positive interpretation of laboratory and operational work for the sponsor. It is therefore indispensable for reconstructing the programme’s own evidential case. `SRC-CIA-PAT-1975`

It is not independent confirmation. Contractor reports must be read with complete trial accounting, later criticisms, replication attempts, the NRC review, and the AIR closeout. “The report says the results were operationally significant” and “the results were independently shown to be operationally significant” are different claims.

### 3.3 Puthoff and Targ, Proceedings of the IEEE 1976

The 1976 review described remote geographical and technical target experiments, blind judging, long-distance trials, and shielding tests. The NASA Technical Reports Server preserves a public record of the journal reprint and its claims. `SRC-PUTHOFF-TARG-1976`

### 3.4 The replication and cueing dispute

Marks and Kammann reported duplicate experiments that did not verify the original conclusions. Later exchanges argued about whether transcript cues, judging, and protocol differences explained results. This dispute matters because an evocative free-response transcript is unusually vulnerable to flexible matching. `SRC-MARKS-KAMMANN-1978`, `SRC-TART-PUTHOFF-TARG-1980`, `SRC-SCOTT-1982`

The methodological lesson survives regardless of one’s preferred interpretation:

- transcripts shown to judges must not contain target-order, date, location, or procedural clues;
- judges should see every transcript and target under symmetrical conditions;
- target pools, assignment rules, exclusions, and analysis must be fixed before feedback;
- independent replication is a different claim from repeated work by the same laboratory.

## 4. What declassified sessions show

The archive contains neither uninterrupted success nor uninterrupted failure. A credible research module must sample all three categories below.

### 4.1 Explicitly poor or null sessions

**Session LIII** was a training session whose summary said the target images showed “absolutely no target correlation.” It also documented a protocol deviation: the beacon chose a site rather than using a randomly generated preselected target. `SRC-CIA-LIII-1978`

**Session 876** was another training record whose post-session analysis reported very little correlation. `SRC-CIA-876-1982`

These records are important because they disprove the idea that the archive contains only curated success stories. They also show why “the government documented it” cannot substitute for a measured success rate.

### 4.2 Apparent correspondence in a controlled training setting

**RV-018 / Site 0059** records a 1985 CRV training session targeting the Solar One power plant in the Mojave Desert. The form lists the highest stage as IV and summarizes descriptors including metallic, thin, pointed, tall, open structure, huge, rocky, rough, dry, and antenna-like AOL. `SRC-CIA-RV018-1985`

The record is useful as a worked example of stage notation and feedback. It is not by itself a probability calculation: without the full target pool, assignment process, scoring rule, all session records, and independent judging, one cannot infer how surprising the match was.

### 4.3 The coordinate-error case

**Session XCI** is often treated as a striking anecdote. Its summary says the intended Offutt Air Force Base coordinates were erroneous; the viewer described a meandering body of water on a flat plain with cliffs, which the report associated with the actual coordinate near Pisgah, Iowa. `SRC-CIA-XCI`

This is a valuable discriminating case because it appears, on the report’s own account, to track the coordinate rather than the interviewer’s intended site. It should still be evaluated with the same questions as any hit: when the error was discovered, who knew what, whether the transcript was fixed, how many alternative geographic descriptions fit, and whether the scoring was independent.

### 4.4 Operational raw-data reports

Iran-hostage-era reports such as **CD-58** state that viewer impressions were provided as raw intelligence data, had not undergone intermediate analysis or collation, and left interpretation and use to the requester. `SRC-CIA-CD58-1980`

This wording is a warning, not validation. It shows how the programme represented the product and why later retrospective matching can be dangerous: vague raw descriptions can be reinterpreted after facts become known.

### 4.5 Procedure reports

A 1987 SUN STREAK procedures report documents CRV source identifiers, interviewer tasking, mission status, and requested site configuration and purpose. `SRC-CIA-SUN-STREAK-1987`

Procedure records are strong evidence that structured tasking occurred. They are weaker evidence for accuracy unless paired with target truth, pre-session cueing, full transcript, evaluation criteria, and all comparable sessions.

## 5. The 1988 National Research Council review

The U.S. Army asked the National Research Council to examine a range of human-performance techniques. Its 1988 consensus report included a chapter on paranormal phenomena and concluded that the available record did not provide a sound scientific basis for remote viewing as an enhancement technique. `SRC-NRC-1988`

This review predates some later SAIC experiments emphasized in the 1995 AIR review. It therefore cannot settle later evidence, but it establishes that serious methodological criticism existed before the final programme evaluation.

## 6. The 1995 AIR review: the essential split

The American Institutes for Research review is the most important single source because it evaluated both research and operational use at the point of programme closure. `SRC-CIA-AIR-1995`

### 6.1 What the AIR executive summary said about laboratories

AIR reported that:

- laboratory hits occurred more often than chance;
- the observed effects could not be unambiguously attributed to viewers rather than judges, targets, or other methodological characteristics;
- the experiments had not established the origin or nature of the effect.

This is neither “nothing happened” nor “psychic spying was proven.” It is a finding of statistical departure plus unresolved attribution.

### 6.2 What AIR said about operations

AIR’s operational review found that:

- laboratory conditions did not transfer cleanly to intelligence tasks;
- end users sometimes saw broad background accuracy;
- reports lacked the concrete specifics valued in intelligence;
- information was inconsistent, vague, and required substantial interpretation;
- the review found no case in which the information guided an intelligence operation.

AIR therefore recommended against continued intelligence use.

### 6.3 Utts and Hyman agreed more than the slogans suggest

Jessica Utts concluded that, under standards used in other sciences, psychic functioning had been established and the programme should shift from proof to mechanism and usefulness. `SRC-UTTS-1995`

Ray Hyman agreed that the better SAIC results could not simply be dismissed as chance and were not obviously explained by the early flaws. He rejected the next inferential step: an anomaly did not yet identify a paranormal cause, and independent replication and a predictive theory were insufficient. `SRC-HYMAN-1995`

The durable point of agreement was **a statistical anomaly in the reviewed experiments**. The durable disagreement was **what caused it and whether the evidence justified a paranormal label**.

### 6.4 Five claims AIR keeps separate

| Claim | AIR-era status |
|---|---|
| Some reviewed laboratory scores departed from chance | Supported in the review |
| The departure was caused by a remote-viewing faculty | Disputed / not resolved by AIR |
| CRV’s Matrix or signal-line model explains the effect | Not established |
| The effect can produce reliable target-specific intelligence | Not supported by operational review |
| Further basic research could be scientifically interesting | Compatible with parts of the expert debate, but not a recommendation to continue intelligence operations |

## 7. Evidence after STARGATE

### 7.1 2023 systematic review and meta-analysis

Tressoldi and Katz selected 36 studies with 40 effect sizes through December 2022 and reported an average effect size of 0.34 after outlier exclusion, with no detected publication bias under their analyses. `SRC-TRESSOLDI-KATZ-2023`

Treat that as a **published synthesis claim**, not as automatic resolution. A strong follow-up audit should inspect:

- protocol and outcome heterogeneity;
- dependence among studies, laboratories, viewers, judges, and target pools;
- inclusion and exclusion decisions;
- small-study and selective-reporting sensitivity beyond the tests used;
- whether effect sizes reflect forced-choice judging, free response, precognition, clairvoyance, or mixed paradigms;
- prospective, preregistered, multi-lab replications.

### 7.2 2023 follow-up experiment

Escolà-Gascón published a follow-up test framed against CIA remote-viewing experiments. It belongs in the current source universe as a recent primary study, but one paper does not establish general reliability or mechanism. `SRC-ESCOLA-GASCON-2023`

### 7.3 2026 expert-guideline survey

Akin, Tressoldi, and Katz surveyed eleven experts. The abstract reports provisional agreement on structured training, neutral environments, blinding, and multiple independent viewers, alongside disagreement over method flexibility, meditation, feedback, and individual technique. `SRC-AKIN-TRESSOLDI-KATZ-2026`

This source is useful for generating testable protocol comparisons. It measures expert opinion, not whether those recommendations outperform alternatives.

## 8. Evidence grading used by this module

Each claim should carry two separate grades.

### Source authority

- **A1 — primary official record:** government document, direct experimental paper, preregistration, raw dataset, or exact protocol.
- **A2 — primary scholarly synthesis:** systematic review, consensus review, or formal programme evaluation.
- **B1 — direct participant/practitioner source:** manual, memoir, training material, or expert survey; valuable for method and phenomenology.
- **B2 — scholarly commentary or critique:** analysis without the complete primary dataset.
- **C — secondary narrative:** journalism, encyclopedia, podcast, commercial training page, or unsourced retelling.

### Claim status

- **established_record:** the document/event/method demonstrably exists.
- **supported_in_source:** the cited source reports evidence for the claim, but wider adjudication remains open.
- **contested:** credible sources make materially different evaluations.
- **not_established:** available sources do not justify the claim.
- **rejected_in_review:** a named review explicitly rejected the claim in its scope.
- **unknown:** the necessary evidence has not been located or evaluated.

A primary source can authoritatively establish what its authors did or concluded while still being weak evidence that the conclusion is true.

## 9. What cannot currently be claimed

This module does not claim that:

- programme duration, renaming, secrecy, or spending proves remote viewing worked;
- the public budget was secretly billions of dollars;
- “everyone” can remote view;
- CRV’s Matrix or signal line is an established physical structure;
- double-blind conditions are needed because the viewer would otherwise telepathically read the monitor;
- any shielding material or psychic countermeasure has been demonstrated;
- one impressive transcript proves clairvoyance, telepathy, or precognition;
- the 1995 closure proves every laboratory result was chance;
- the 2023 meta-analysis closes the replication debate;
- current government remote-viewing programmes exist without current public evidence.

Those propositions may be framed as hypotheses or research questions, not facts.

## 10. The research programme that follows from the record

The most informative next work is not another argument from anecdotes. It is a sequence of discriminating tests:

1. reproduce the basic blind target-description paradigm with immutable target commitments;
2. measure inter-judge reliability and automated feature agreement;
3. compare sender versus no-sender trials;
4. compare target-before-session with target-after-transcript-lock trials;
5. compare CRV stage training with an equally intensive descriptive-observation control;
6. preregister all exclusions and publish every trial;
7. repeat across independent laboratories using shared code but separately generated target pools;
8. test whether any above-chance effect predicts target-specific information rather than broad gestalt;
9. separate viewer skill, target entropy, judge effects, and feedback effects in a hierarchical model;
10. attempt prospective operational-style tasks only after laboratory reliability is independently demonstrated.

That programme honors the possibility premise by taking it seriously enough to test cleanly.
